# The kit dresses a character in dresses, blazers, cloaks, hoods, hoodies, robes, kimonos and aprons

- A coat framework cuts a garment from a bodice welded to sleeves and a skirt hung from the waist seam, over whatever is worn under it. The kit's dress, blazer, cloak and robe are built on it.
- The dress (#328) has a neckline, sleeves and skirt of its own; the blazer (#329) notch or shawl lapels, hip and breast pockets, buttons and a vent; the cloak (#330) hangs from the shoulders over the arms, fastened at the neck by clasps and a chain.
- A hood (#325) goes on a cloak or a robe, folded down on the back or raised over the head and hair.
- The hoodie (#392) is a pullover with knitted rib cuffs and hem, a kangaroo pocket, drawstrings with aglets and the hood.
- The kimono (#393) wraps left over right, with a collar band, an under-robe's collar in the V, wide sleeves with hanging pouches from short to furisode length, and an obi (#212) with a drum or butterfly bow and a cord.
- The robe (#324, #334, #384, #194) has bell sleeves, a patterned front band, clasps and a hood. Damask and interlace are new woven patterns for any garment's fabric.
- The apron (#394) ties over a dress or a skirt at the waist, with a bib, straps crossed at the back, a bow and a frilled hem; a frilled headdress rides over the hair of any hairstyle.
- Every garment passes the conformance suite on each template and body shape: it clears the body by half its ease, never meets the body mesh, and has exactly the open borders it documents.
